The framework

Three different things can increase your wealth. Only one is cash.

Cash flow

Rent left after modeled operating costs and mortgage payments. This is the part that can actually reach your bank account.

Principal paydown

Part of each mortgage payment reduces what you owe. It does not arrive as cash, but it increases your equity dollar for dollar.

Appreciation

A change in the property's market value. It is uncertain and generally becomes spendable only after a sale or refinance.

Why count principal separately from cash flow?

The full mortgage payment is deducted when we calculate cash flow. But part of that payment is not an economic expense: it reduces your debt. Your bank balance does not rise, yet your equity does.

What exactly is appreciation?

Appreciation is simply a change in market value. It is not rent and nobody deposits it into your checking account. We keep it separate because it is an assumption, not current cash flow.

What this teaches

Lease-up can take longer than hoped. Cash flow should be judged with vacancy built in, not by assuming twelve perfect rent checks. A track record is more useful when it includes the inconvenient cases as well as the easy ones.
